The latest in Beaver Lake

November 5, 2026

Beaver Lake Park covers 83 acres with two entrances: the lake side at 25101 SE 24th Street, and the athletic-field side at 2526 244th Avenue SE, where three sports fields, a playground and an off-leash dog park sit. The site began as a 1930s resort, served as Camp Cabrini for the Catholic Youth Organization until 1985, and reached the city in 2003. Park page

October 29, 2026

The lake's main basin covers 62 acres, averages 21 feet deep and reaches 54 feet, per King County's lake data. 2026 is one of the Beaver Lake Management District's whole-lake monitoring years, after 2021; the district, begun in 1996, also samples the streams monthly. King County lake data · Management district

October 22, 2026

Beaver Lake Park is on winter hours: 7:30 AM to dusk from October through March. The 83-acre park began as a private resort in the 1930s, became the Catholic Youth Organization's Camp Cabrini from 1960 to 1985, then a King County park, and passed to the city in 2003. The lake's public ramp allows electric motors only. Park page

October 15, 2026

Council renewed the Beaver Lake Management District in July for 2027 through 2036. The assessment roll that went to a council hearing September 1 would raise $733,390 over those ten years: $320 a year for each of 126 waterfront parcels and $33 a year for each of 1,012 other parcels inside the district. Hearing notice

October 12, 2026

The Beaver Lake Management District Advisory Board meets Monday, October 19, 5:30 to 7 PM, at City Hall and on Zoom. The board decides lake treatment, such as fragrant water lily control, paid for by an annual assessment on lakefront and draining properties. Meeting · Water lily FAQ

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Sammamish newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Sammamish that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Sammamish's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
