The latest in Pine Lake

November 5, 2026

Pine Lake Park is 19 wooded acres at 2401 228th Avenue SE, with a beach, lifeguarded swimming, a boat launch and a picnic shelter you can reserve. From October through March it opens at 7:30 AM and closes at dusk. The land was a resort in the 1910s, King County bought it for a park in the mid-1960s, and it came to the city when Sammamish incorporated in 1999. Park page

October 29, 2026

Big Rock Park Central, parking at 1516 220th Ave SE (12 stalls), is 20 acres of forest and meadow with over 1.5 miles of trails, the Reard House and a heritage garden. Its Treehouse opens to visitors four times a month from March through October, so this is its last week of the season. The land came from Mary Pigott's gift of three parcels. Park page

October 22, 2026

The same draft priorities ask the state for $750,000 to repair the SE 24th Street boardwalk next to Discovery Elementary, the walkway linking the school with nearby streets and parks. The full repair is estimated at $3.3 million: design in 2026, construction in 2027 and 2028. October 20 packet

October 15, 2026

Pine Lake Park, 2401 228th Ave SE, began as a private resort in the 1910s; King County bought it in the mid-1960s and it passed to the city at incorporation in 1999. Fishing season on the lake closes October 31, and only electric motors are allowed. Park page

October 12, 2026

Repaving of Issaquah-Pine Lake Road SE between 228th and 234th Avenues SE is done; markings, signs and signal-loop work continue through about October 19, weather permitting. A $7.65 million remodel of Fire Station 83, 3425 Issaquah-Pine Lake Road SE, was up for council approval October 6, with work starting this fall and finishing in early 2028 if approved. Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Sammamish newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Sammamish that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Sammamish's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It goes to a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher only if you tick the sharing box on the form; that brokerage may then contact you about your home. Leave the box empty and it stays with us. Never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
