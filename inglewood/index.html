The latest in Inglewood

November 12, 2026

The city is taking bids through December 3 for the Demery Hill Vault Retrofit near NE 8th Street and 218th Place NE: an underground water quality treatment vault, new stormwater pipes, paving and roadside restoration. Construction cannot start before May 1, 2027, because the vault has a long order time. Invitation to bid

November 5, 2026

NE Inglewood Hill Road is one of six designated access points King County lists on the trail through Sammamish. The others are 187th Avenue NE, NE 7th Court, SE Eighth Street, SE 33rd Street and 212th Way SE. The city's trail page says trail users may not cross private driveways or property to reach it. Trail page

October 29, 2026

The Eastlake Community Fields, 400 228th Ave NE, are lit artificial-turf fields for baseball, football, lacrosse, soccer and softball, open to the community on weekday evenings, weekends and summers under an agreement with the Lake Washington School District. Fields 1 and 2 opened in January 2006; Field 3 went to turf in 2013. Park page

October 22, 2026

The city's draft 2027 state legislative priorities ask for $500,000 to close the last sidewalk gap on Inglewood Hill Road, which would make the walk continuous from East Lake Sammamish Parkway to 228th Avenue SE. Estimated cost is $1.7 million, with design in 2027 and construction in 2028. October 20 packet

October 15, 2026

East Sammamish Park's ball fields got their first major renovation since the park was built in the early 1990s: synthetic turf infields, turf in the batting cages and ADA upgrades between the fields and the entry. The council's calendar lists project acceptance on November 4. Project · Council calendar

October 12, 2026

Stormwater-pipe camera work brings occasional single-lane closures on 222nd Place NE at NE 16th Street through Friday. (evergreen) East Sammamish Park, 21300 NE 16th Street, is 19 acres with ball fields, tennis and pickleball courts and a one-acre native-forest restoration; fall hours are 7:30 AM to dusk. Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Sammamish newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Sammamish that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Sammamish's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
