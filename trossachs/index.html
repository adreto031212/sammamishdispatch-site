The latest in Trossachs

November 5, 2026

Soaring Eagle Regional Park is 790 acres of forest, wetlands and wildlife habitat at 26015 E Main Drive, with 12 miles of multi-use trails. Its south entry is at the end of Trossachs Boulevard, reached from Southeast Duthie Hill Road, and the county lists parking along Trossachs Boulevard at the south end of the park. The main entry is on the west side, off 228th Avenue SE and SE 8th Street. (evergreen) King County

October 29, 2026

Duthie Hill Park, 26300 SE Issaquah-Fall City Road, is 120 acres of King County forest with a six-mile network of one-way single-track trails laid out like a four-leaf clover, downhill lines inside each leaf and perimeter trails for hikers and runners. The 74-car lot is on the north side of the road. King County Parks

October 22, 2026

Beaver Lake Preserve, 1400 West Beaver Lake Drive SE, has 76 acres and 1.35 miles of trails, including a forested loop, with connections to Soaring Eagle Park and the Hazel Wolf Wetlands Preserve. The link to Soaring Eagle came with 17 acres bought from the Kipper family in 2007. Ten-car gravel lot. Park page

October 15, 2026

The south entrance to Soaring Eagle Regional Park sits at the end of Trossachs Boulevard, off SE Duthie Hill Road, with parking along the boulevard. The county park is 790 acres with 12 miles of multi-use trails open to hiking, horses and mountain bikes. King County Parks

October 12, 2026

Beaver Lake Preserve, 1400 West Beaver Lake Drive SE, is 76 acres split by the road, with 1.35 miles of trails and a link to Soaring Eagle Park through 17 acres bought in 2007. A native-planting work party runs Saturday, November 7, 11 AM to 2 PM; sign-up is required because the gravel lot holds ten cars. Preserve · Work party

Questions people ask

Is this a real estate newsletter?

No. It is a Sammamish newsletter that happens to know the housing market. Three of the four sections are about living here. The fourth tells you what homes are selling for, because in Sammamish that is news too.

Who writes it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publisher. Every item comes from Sammamish's own public record (council agendas, permit notices, school district and transit updates, parks calendars) and links its source. It is researched and written with AI tools, and an editor reads every issue before it goes out.

How often, and is it really free?

Once a week, Thursday at 6 AM, about five minutes to read. Free, no paywall, unsubscribe in one click at the bottom of any issue.

Who owns it?

Neighborhood Press LLC, an independent publishing company. The newsletter is free, and no sponsor buys coverage. Our standards.

What happens with my address?

It is used for one thing: a monthly estimate of what your home would sell for today. It is shared with a local real estate brokerage under the same ownership as our publisher, which may contact you about your home, and is never sold or posted. Remove it any time by replying to any issue.
